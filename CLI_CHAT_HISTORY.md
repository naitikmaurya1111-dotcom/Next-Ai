### Section 1: Electrostatics & Current Electricity

#### 1. Gauss's Law (Integral & Differential Forms)
$$\oint_S \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{A} = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0} \iff \nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$$
* **Parameters**: $\mathbf{E}$ is electric field, $d\mathbf{A}$ is outward normal area element, $Q_{\text{enc}}$ is enclosed charge, $\rho$ is local charge density.
* **Critical Trap**: Applies strictly to closed surfaces. Solving for $\mathbf{E}$ analytically requires geometric symmetry (spherical, cylindrical, or planar); otherwise, it determines only total flux, not local field strength.

#### 2. Short Electric Dipole Field and Potential
$$\mathbf{E}_{\text{axial}} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2\mathbf{p}}{r^3}, \quad \mathbf{E}_{\text{eq}} \approx -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf{p}}{r^3}, \quad V(r, \theta) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf{p} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{r}}}{r^2}$$
* **Parameters**: $\mathbf{p} = q(2\mathbf{a})$ is the dipole moment directed from $-q$ to $+q$, $r$ is distance from dipole center.
* **Validity Limit**: Assumes $r \gg 2a$. For near-field points ($r \sim a$), the full finite-length Coulomb superposition must be applied.

#### 3. Energy Density & Capacitor Charge-Sharing Energy Loss
$$u_E = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2, \quad \Delta U = \frac{C_1 C_2}{2(C_1 + C_2)}(V_1 - V_2)^2$$
* **Parameters**: $u_E$ is electrostatic energy per unit volume; $\Delta U$ is dissipation during charge redistribution between isolated capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$.
* **Critical Insight**: $\Delta U$ is independent of connecting wire resistance; energy is dissipated as electromagnetic radiation and Joule heating regardless of path resistance.

#### 4. Microscopic Ohm's Law & Drift Mechanics
$$\mathbf{J} = \sigma \mathbf{E} = n e \mathbf{v}_d, \quad \mathbf{v}_d = -\frac{e\tau}{m_e}\mathbf{E}$$
* **Parameters**: $\mathbf{J}$ is current density vector, $\sigma = \frac{n e^2 \tau}{m_e}$ is electrical conductivity, $\tau$ is mean relaxation time, $n$ is charge carrier density.
* **Validity Limit**: Assumes low electric fields where drift speed is negligible compared to Fermi thermal speed ($v_d \ll v_{\text{th}}$). Fails at high fields due to velocity saturation.

### Section 2: Magnetostatics & Electrodynamics

#### 5. Biot-Savart Law (Axial Circular Loop Field)
$$d\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I(d\mathbf{l} \times \hat{\mathbf{r}})}{r^2} \implies B_{\text{axis}} = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$$
* **Parameters**: $R$ is loop radius, $x$ is axial distance from the center, $N$ is number of turns.
* **Boundary Case**: At $x = 0$, $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2R}$. For $x \gg R$, it reduces to magnetic dipole field $B \approx \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{2m}{x^3}$, where $m = N I (\pi R^2)$.

#### 6. Lorentz Force & Magnetic Torque
$$\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}), \quad \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m} \times \mathbf{B} = N I (\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B})$$
* **Cyclotron Frequency**: $\omega_c = \frac{qB}{m}$ (governs helical trajectories).
* **Validity Limit**: Cyclotron frequency expression is strictly non-relativistic ($v \ll c$). When $v \to c$, relativistic mass increase requires synchro-cyclotron phase tuning.

#### 7. Faraday-Lenz Law & Motional EMF
$$\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}, \quad \mathcal{E}_{\text{motional}} = \oint (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}) \cdot d\mathbf{l}$$
* **Parameters**: $\Phi_B = \iint \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{A}$ is magnetic flux. For a straight conductor translating perpendicularly through uniform $B$: $\mathcal{E} = Bvl$.
* **Sign Convention**: The negative sign accounts for energy conservation (Lenz's Law); induced current opposes the flux variation driving it.

#### 8. Self and Mutual Inductance
$$\mathcal{E} = -L\frac{dI}{dt}, \quad L_{\text{solenoid}} = \mu_0 n^2 A l, \quad M = k\sqrt{L_1 L_2}$$
* **Parameters**: $n = N/l$ is turns per unit length, $k \in [0, 1]$ is magnetic coupling coefficient.
* **Magnetic Energy Storage**: $U_B = \frac{1}{2}LI^2$; energy density in magnetic field is $u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$.

### Section 3: Alternating Currents & Electromagnetic Waves

#### 9. Series LCR Impedance, Resonance, and Quality Factor
$$Z = \sqrt{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}, \quad \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}, \quad Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} = \frac{\omega_0}{2\Delta\omega}$$
* **Phase Angle**: $\tan\phi = \frac{\omega L - 1/(\omega C)}{R}$. Current leads voltage when capacitive ($\omega < \omega_0$) and lags when inductive ($\omega > \omega_0$).
* **Critical Trap**: At series resonance ($\omega = \omega_0$), impedance is minimum ($Z = R$), and voltage drops across $L$ and $C$ can exceed supply voltage by factor $Q$.

#### 10. AC Real Power and Power Factor
$$P_{\text{avg}} = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}} \cos\phi, \quad \text{Power Factor} = \cos\phi = \frac{R}{Z}$$
* **Wattless Current**: $I_{\text{wattless}} = I_{\text{rms}}\sin\phi$. In purely reactive circuits ($R = 0 \implies \phi = \pm \pi/2$), zero net work is done per cycle despite large circulating currents.

#### 11. Maxwell-Ampère Equation & EM Wave Relation
$$\oint \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0 \left(I_C + \varepsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}\right), \quad c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}} = \frac{E_0}{B_0}$$
* **Parameters**: $\varepsilon_0 \frac{d\Phi_E}{dt} = I_D$ (displacement current). In vacuum, fields propagate transversely with Poynting vector $\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B})$.

### Section 4: Ray & Wave Optics

#### 12. Lens Maker's Equation & Combination of Thin Lenses
$$\frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{\mu_{\text{lens}}}{\mu_{\text{medium}}} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right), \quad P = \sum_i P_i \iff \frac{1}{F} = \sum_i \frac{1}{f_i}$$
* **Sign Convention**: Cartesian coordinate standard ($R > 0$ if center of curvature lies in $+x$ direction of incident light).
* **Failure Mode**: When lenses are separated by distance $d$, combination focal length becomes $\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2} - \frac{d}{f_1 f_2}$.

#### 13. Prism Formula (Minimum Deviation)
$$\mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}$$
* **Parameters**: $A$ is prism apex angle, $D_m$ is angle of minimum deviation.
* **Small Angle Limit**: For thin prisms ($A \le 10^\circ$), $\sin\theta \approx \theta \implies \delta \approx (\mu - 1)A$.

#### 14. Young’s Double Slit Interference (YDSE)
$$\beta = \frac{\lambda D}{d}, \quad I(\Delta x) = 4I_0 \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi d y}{\lambda D}\right)$$
* **Parameters**: $d$ is slit separation, $D$ is slit-to-screen distance, $\beta$ is fringe width. Path difference $\Delta x = \frac{yd}{D}$.
* **Approximation Limits**: Requires $d \ll D$ and $y \ll D$ (paraxial limit). If light undergoes medium transition, $\beta \to \beta/\mu$.

#### 15. Single-Slit Fraunhofer Diffraction & Brewster's Law
$$a \sin\theta = m\lambda \quad (m \in \{\pm 1, \pm 2, \dots\}), \quad \tan\theta_p = \mu$$
* **Angular Width of Central Maximum**: $2\theta_0 \approx \frac{2\lambda}{a}$.
* **Polarization**: At Brewster's angle $\theta_p$, reflected ray is completely plane-polarized perpendicular to plane of incidence, and reflected ray $\perp$ refracted ray.

### Section 5: Modern Physics

#### 16. Einstein’s Photoelectric Equation
$$K_{\max} = eV_s = h\nu - \Phi_0 = hc\left(\frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{1}{\lambda_0}\right)$$
* **Parameters**: $V_s$ is stopping potential, $\Phi_0 = h\nu_0$ is work function, $\nu_0$ is threshold frequency.
* **Key Insight**: $K_{\max}$ depends linearly on frequency $\nu$, strictly independent of incident beam intensity. Intensity controls only saturation photocurrent.

#### 17. de Broglie Wavelength Relations
$$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mK}} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}}$$
* **Electron under Potential $V$**:
  $$\lambda_e \approx \frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}} \text{ nm} = \sqrt{\frac{150}{V}} \text{ \AA}$$
* **Thermal Neutrons**: $\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3m k_B T}}$ ($K = \frac{3}{2}k_B T$).

#### 18. Bohr Model (Hydrogen-like Atoms, Atomic Number $Z$)
$$r_n = \frac{n^2 h^2 \varepsilon_0}{\pi m Z e^2} \approx 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z} \text{ \AA}, \quad E_n = -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8\varepsilon_0^2 h^2 n^2} \approx -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2} \text{ eV}$$
* **Spectral Emission (Rydberg Equation)**:
  $$\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_\infty Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)$$
* **Limitation**: Strictly valid only for one-electron species ($\text{H}, \text{He}^+, \text{Li}^{2+}$). Fails for multi-electron systems due to electron-electron Coulomb repulsion.

#### 19. Nuclear Binding Energy & Mass Defect
$$\Delta E_b = \Delta m \cdot c^2 = \left[Z m_p + (A - Z)m_n - M_{\text{nucleus}}(A, Z)\right] \times 931.5 \text{ MeV/u}$$
* **Stability Metric**: Peak of $\Delta E_b / A \approx 8.8\text{ MeV/nucleon}$ occurs near $^{56}\text{Fe}$ and $^{62}\text{Ni}$. Lighter nuclei undergo fusion; heavy nuclei ($A > 230$) undergo fission to achieve higher binding per nucleon.

#### 20. Law of Radioactive Decay
$$N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}, \quad T_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\lambda} \approx \frac{0.693}{\lambda}, \quad \tau = \frac{1}{\lambda}$$
* **Activity Relation**: $A(t) = -\frac{dN}{dt} = \lambda N(t) = A_0 e^{-\lambda t}$.
* **Statistical Constraint**: Applicable only for statistically large ensembles ($N \gg 1$). Fails when sample population decays to discrete single-particle events.
